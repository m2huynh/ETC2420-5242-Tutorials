Week 5

Estimation

Introduction

This week we will begin by taking a sample of households from a fictitious town to estimate weekly_income.

  1. Begin by allocating points to create your field surveyor. You only have 10 points to allocate, so think carefully about the kind of surveyor you want to build. Some choices may help you reach more households, while others may help you collect cleaner information.
  2. Once you have allocated your points, click the Build surveyor button.
  3. On the next screen, click the Take sample button. This will provide you with a random household from this town. You will also get an estimate for the mean income. How good this estimate is will depend on how you created your surveyor.

NoteQuestion 3

Suppose the target parameter is the true mean weekly income of all households in the town.

  1. What is the parameter?
  2. What is the statistic?
  3. What is your point estimate?
ImportantClick for solution

The parameter is the true mean weekly income of all households in the town. We could write this as:

\[ \mu \]

The statistic is the sample mean:

\[ \bar{x} \]

Your point estimate is:

mean(my_sample)

NoteQuestion 7

Compare your result with another student.

  1. Did you get the same sample size?
  2. Did you get the same point estimate?
  3. Did you get the same confidence interval?
  4. Why or why not?
ImportantClick for sample answer

Different students will usually get different samples. Because the sample is different, the sample mean, standard error, and confidence interval will also differ. This is not a contradiction. It is sampling variation. The population is the same town, but each student observes only one part of it. Each sample gives one estimate of the unknown population mean.

NoteQuestion 12

Is the regional comparison an independent-samples problem or a paired-samples problem? Explain.

ImportantClick for solution

This is an independent-samples problem.

A capital city household is not naturally matched to a particular regional household. The two groups are separate.

The parameter is:

\[ \mu_{\text{capital}} - \mu_{\text{regional}} \]

The point estimate is:

\[ \bar{x}_{\text{capital}} - \bar{x}_{\text{regional}} \]
