Week 9

Introduction

Suppose a coffee company is testing a new drink. Before adding it to the menu, the company wants to know whether customers genuinely support the launch. Rather than asking for a single opinion, the company collects short customer comments from taste-testing sessions. Some comments clearly support launching the drink. Others are negative, uncertain, or only mildly positive.

Your task is to act as the product team. Each day, you will read a small set of customer comments and decide which ones count as genuine support for launching the drink.

For each day:

  • Tick the comments that you think count as genuine support.
  • The app will count how many comments you selected.
  • That count becomes the data for the Bayesian update.

Part 1: Customer feedback and Bayesian updating

In the app, you acted as the product team for a coffee company deciding whether to launch a new drink.

Each day, you read six customer comments and selected the comments that counted as genuine support for launching the drink. The app recorded the number of selected comments as:

\[ x = \text{number of comments judged as genuine support} \]

Each day had:

\[ n = 6 \]

We will use your copied data from the app to reproduce the Bayesian updating process in R.

The unknown parameter is:

\[ \theta = \text{the proportion of customers who genuinely support launching the drink} \]

We begin with a neutral prior:

\[ \theta \sim \text{Beta}(1,1) \]

After observing (x) supportive comments out of (n), the posterior is:

\[ \theta \mid x \sim \text{Beta}(\alpha + x,\ \beta + n - x) \]

The posterior from one day becomes the prior for the next day.

The value prob_above_half answers the question:

After six days of customer feedback, how likely is it that more than half of customers genuinely support launching the drink?

A value close to 1 provides strong evidence in favour of launch support being above 50%. A value close to 0 suggests weak evidence that support is above 50%. A value near 0.5 means there is still substantial uncertainty.
