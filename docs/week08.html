In this tutorial we will begin by investigating likelihood and fitting models using the app below. Your tutor will explain to you how to use it.

Introduction

In the opening activity, you selected probability distributions and adjusted their parameters until they appeared to fit the observed data. You were informally searching for the combination of model and parameter values that made the data most likely.

In this tutorial, R will perform that search for us. We will use:

  • MASS::fitdistr() to obtain maximum likelihood estimates;
  • the fitted log-likelihood to compare candidate models; and
  • Q–Q plots to investigate how well each fitted distribution describes the data.

Scenario

A hospital is investigating patient activity in its emergency department. For each of 200 patients, two variables were recorded:

  • waiting_time: the number of minutes before the patient was seen;
  • tests_ordered: the number of diagnostic tests ordered for the patient.

The hospital wants to identify probability models that adequately describe these variables. Appropriate models could later be used for simulation, planning and estimating the probability of unusually long waits or unusually large numbers of tests.

NoteQuestion 9

In two or three sentences, recommend:

  1. a probability model for patient waiting times; and
  2. a probability model for the number of tests ordered.

Support each recommendation using the fitted log-likelihood and Q–Q plot.

ImportantClick for solution

A gamma distribution is a suitable model for patient waiting times because it has a relatively large fitted log-likelihood and its Q–Q plot follows the reference line reasonably closely. A negative binomial distribution is suitable for the number of tests because it fits the overdispersed counts better than Poisson according to both its log-likelihood and Q–Q plot.

These models are the strongest choices among those considered; the diagnostics do not prove that either is the true data-generating distribution.
